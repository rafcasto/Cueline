# Tertulia

[tertulia.cloud](https://tertulia.cloud) (formerly Cueline). Webinars with the engagement built in. The presenter uploads a deck as a PDF, presents it in the room, and launches polls, quizzes, word clouds and calls to action as overlays for everyone watching. Each activity can carry a timer; when it runs out the presenter reveals the results as an overlay. Participants react at any time and see a waiting room before the session starts.

The full design is in [docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md); a feature and landing-page comparison with Univid is in [docs/COMPETITOR-UNIVID.md](docs/COMPETITOR-UNIVID.md); tiers, costs and capacity on the self-hosted LiveKit node are in [docs/BUSINESS-MODEL.md](docs/BUSINESS-MODEL.md). This repo is the Phase 1 build: rooms the host creates (each with its own events, email sequence, deck, activities and waiting room), presenters on WebRTC, viewers on HLS, PDF deck, activities, Q&A, reactions, waiting room, recording, plus the Phase 3 tiers and billing and self-serve host sign-up.

## Layout

| Path | What |
| --- | --- |
| `supabase/migrations` | Schema. Every table carries `org_id`; session tables carry `session_id`. RLS, the `session:{id}` broadcast trigger, timer and quiz-scoring triggers, `run_tallies` and `quiz_scores` views, storage buckets, room email sequences and their inheritance by events. |
| `supabase/tests` | Stubs + smoke test that exercise the migration against plain Postgres (`pnpm db:test`). |
| `apps/web` | Next.js on Vercel: landing page (`/`), the public directory (`/explore`, `/explore/[slug]`), invite link and registration (`/join/[token]`), the typed door (`/join`, email + code) and the personal link (`/a/[token]`), participant room (`/r/[slug]`), the host's rooms (`/host`) and the host panel per room (`/host/[slug]`), the platform admin portal (`/admin`), and the functions that need the service key (`/api/*`). |

## How it fits together

**Landing page.** The community front door, on the Meetup pattern and with its restraint (a white page, one big promise, whitespace and type for hierarchy, no panels or product mocks): a search box into Explore, the next public events and the listed rooms (read live from the directory; empty states when there are none or it cannot be reached), topic chips, how it works for someone joining and for someone organising, the plans, and *Start a community* as the host door. Google Analytics 4 (`components/Analytics`, measurement ID in `NEXT_PUBLIC_GA_MEASUREMENT_ID`) loads from the root layout in production builds only; every call to action carries a `data-track` value that is reported as a `cta_click` event. Attendees who already registered join with the email and the six-character code from their welcome email, or skip the typing with the personal link in that email.

**Rooms.** A host creates rooms from `/host`. A room is a standing space: its own plan, events and invite links, email sequence, deck, activities, waiting room and branding. The plan belongs to the room: an organisation's first room is free, upgrading it makes it a Starter or Pro room, and every further room is bought as its own Starter or Pro subscription from `/host/billing`. A free room never sits next to a paid one: a second room cannot be added until the first is upgraded, and a paid room whose subscription lapses while the organisation has other rooms is locked (cannot open, go live or take new events) until it is paid again or the others are deleted down to one (`roomAccess()` in `apps/web/src/lib/billing.ts`). Platform admins are exempt from all of this: the rooms they create are complimentary, run at Pro, and are never capped or locked. On the way in the host says what the room is about, picking topics the way a Meetup group picks categories (`TopicPicker`, suggestions in `apps/web/src/lib/topics.ts`), so it lands under the right filters on Explore; the Settings tab changes the topics, renames the room, changes its `/r/` address, sets its branding, or deletes it (which cancels the reminders still scheduled for its registrants).

**Branding.** Per room (`rooms.branding`, helpers in `apps/web/src/lib/branding.ts`): an accent colour, a logo and a cover picture. The cover picture (same `media` bucket as the logo) runs across the room's card on Explore and the top of its public page; a room without one gets a band in its accent with its mark, so the grid stays even. The accent is applied as CSS variables on the room and on the invite page, so every button, badge, chosen answer and result bar takes the room's colour; the text on it flips to dark for light colours. The logo (PNG/JPEG/WebP/GIF in the `media` bucket) replaces the product mark in the room header and sits by the room name on the invite page. The Settings tab previews everything with the real components before saving; `/api/rooms` validates the colour and the links. Calls to action are activities (below), not branding; a `cta` left on an older branding row is ignored.

**Invite link and registration.** The organiser creates an event (title, start time, blurb, seats) in the host panel, which is also an invite link. Attendees open `/join/<token>`, see how many seats are left, register with a name and email, and the same page becomes the door on the day: its button follows the session state. `/r/<slug>` sends anyone without a seat to the room's current link; org members walk in. Registrations bind to the attendee's anonymous identity; the personal link (`/a/<token>`) and the email + code form re-bind it, so a second device works. The host panel shows *seated*, *waiting* and *joined*. The reasoning and what to measure are in [docs/INVITE-FLOW.md](docs/INVITE-FLOW.md).

**Seats, the waiting list and opting out.** Each event has the seats the host gives it, up to the plan's participants per session (`invites.capacity`; blank means the plan's maximum, and a downgrade shrinks every event without unseating anyone). `register_attendee()` seats a registrant while seats remain and puts them on the waiting list otherwise, under a lock on the event so the last seat is never given twice; the join page tells someone their place in line, and the Explore card shows *Full · waiting list*. Every email carries `{{cancel_url}}` (`/a/<token>/cancel`): a registrant who is not coming frees their seat there or from the event page, their pending emails are pulled back, and `promote_waitlist()` moves the first person in line into the seat and sends them the welcome and reminders. More seats, or a bigger plan, fill from the waiting list the same way. Helpers in `apps/web/src/lib/seats.ts`.

**Event emails.** Each room carries an email sequence: a welcome sent on registration with the personal link and code (or a waiting-list note when the seats are gone), then reminders at 48 hours, 24 hours, 2 hours and 15 minutes before the start, each asking anyone who is not coming to free their seat. Every event created in the room inherits the sequence, and the send times are counted back from that event's start. Reminders are a paid feature: the free plan sends the welcome and the waiting-list note only, and the Emails tab shows the reminders locked. The host edits copy and timing once per room in the Emails tab; the change reaches every open event and reschedules it at Resend, and changing an event's start time moves its reminders. Details in [docs/EMAILS.md](docs/EMAILS.md).

**Explore.** A public directory at `/explore`, the way Meetup lists groups and events: upcoming public events across every listed room (search, topic chips, people going, register in one tap) and the rooms themselves, each with a public page at `/explore/<slug>` showing its description, topics, upcoming and past events and a way in when it is live. A host lists a room from its Settings tab (public listing, description, up to six topics) and ticks *List on Explore* on each event they want shown; an event is only listed while its room is. Visitors read two views (`public_rooms`, `public_events`) that expose published rows and counts and nothing else; helpers in `apps/web/src/lib/explore.ts`.

**Plans and billing.** Free, Starter and Pro with the caps in [docs/BUSINESS-MODEL.md](docs/BUSINESS-MODEL.md), one subscription per room, paid through Stripe Checkout from `/host/billing` (`rooms.plan`, `rooms.plan_status`; the subscription and its period in `room_subscriptions`, which attendees cannot read; the Stripe customer on the organisation). The plan is enforced when a room is created or opened (first room free, the rest bought; a locked room does not open), when an event's seats are set (up to the room's participants), when registrations queue their emails (reminders on paid rooms only), when a session goes live (the room's sessions this month, audience cap on the LiveKit room, recording only where included, a hard stop on length) and when attendees ask for a token. Recordings and the Insights tab are Pro features (`recording`, `insights` in `PLAN_LIMITS`). Both Stripe modes are configured at once and a platform admin (`pnpm admin:create … --super`) switches new payments between the sandbox and the live account on the billing page or the admin portal. Wiring in [docs/STRIPE.md](docs/STRIPE.md).

**Admin portal.** `/admin`, for platform admins only (anyone else is sent to `/host`): host accounts with their organisations and last sign-in, every organisation with its rooms and their plans, registrations (total, last 30 days, the latest 25), sessions run and live now, every recording on the platform with a *Delete* that removes the files now and marks the session the way the sweep does (`POST /api/admin/recordings`), and the Stripe mode switch. A platform admin sees a *Host · Admin* switch in the header of both portals. Everything is read with the service client after `isPlatformAdmin()`.

**Sign-up and sign-in.** Every *start* button on the site opens `/signup`: organisation name, email, password (`POST /api/auth/signup`, helpers in `apps/web/src/lib/signup.ts`). The organisation is made at once with the new host as owner, so `/host` has somewhere to put the first, free room; if the Supabase project asks for email confirmation the page says so and the host is signed in from the link (`/auth/callback`), otherwise they are signed in on the way out. A taken address is sent to the login page. A host who is signed in but belongs to no organisation (an account made by hand) names one on `/host` (`POST /api/orgs`). Hosts sign in at `/login` with email and password through `POST /api/auth/login`, which signs in on the server so the session cookie arrives as a `Set-Cookie` header (Safari caps script-written cookies at seven days; a header-set cookie keeps its full life). The proxy (`src/proxy.ts`) refreshes the cookie on every request, so a host stays signed in until they sign out; a signed-in host who opens `/login` or `/signup` is sent on to `/host` (or `?next=`), and the public header shows them a *Dashboard* button (`HeaderAccount`, read in the browser so the public pages stay cacheable) in place of *Log in* and *Host an event*.

**Video room.** LiveKit Cloud behind `apps/web/src/lib/video.ts`. Everyone joins the room over WebRTC: presenters and promoted participants publish, the audience subscribes only (`LiveViewer`), so they hear and see the presenters with sub-second latency. One web egress per session films the attendee stage at `/record/[session]` (deck page, presenter video where the layout puts it, activity overlay; the page is let in by a key derived from the LiveKit secret) and writes the recording and an HLS playlist; the playlist is the fallback (`HlsPlayer`, shown edge to edge since it carries the whole stage) when the room cannot take a participant or a join keeps failing, and sits 6-10 s behind. The egress browser must be able to reach the app at `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL` (or `RECORDING_PAGE_BASE_URL`, a tunnel, for local runs). Hosts on Pro download recordings from the Recordings tab; each is kept for the plan's retention (`recordingDays`) and removed by the nightly `/api/recordings/sweep` cron, which marks the session; a platform admin can delete one earlier from `/admin`. It takes a few seconds to appear after "Go live", so the player keeps asking for it (and reconnects after a drop) instead of giving up; where the browser refuses autoplay with sound either view starts muted and shows *Tap for sound*. The bucket behind `RECORDING_PUBLIC_BASE_URL` must allow cross-origin GETs from the app and must not cache `.m3u8` files, or hls.js can neither fetch nor follow the live playlist. Promote-to-stage is `stage_roster` state (`viewer → invited → live → revoked`).

**Deck.** A PDF in the `presentations` storage bucket. The presenter steps through it (arrow keys or buttons); each navigation inserts a `slide_events` row and every participant renders that page from the same PDF with pdf.js.

**Stage layout.** Where the presenter video sits against the deck page, chosen by the host in the live panel: *Corner* (the deck with the camera bottom-right, the default), *Talking head* (the camera fills the stage, the deck is hidden), *Side by side* (deck left, camera right, half each) or *Custom* (the host drags the camera anywhere on the stage and resizes it from its corner; arrow keys nudge it, plus and minus resize it). The host's stage is the same component attendees see (`StageCanvas`), around the host's own camera, so what the host sees is what attendees get; dragging the camera in any preset switches to custom from where it was. The layout is one `jsonb` on the session (`sessions.layout`, helpers in `apps/web/src/lib/stage-layout.ts`), so a change rides the session broadcast and attendees follow at once; the room keeps the last choice (`rooms.stage_layout`) as the starting layout of the next session. Geometry is in percent of a 16:9 stage, where a 16:9 box is as tall as it is wide, so a custom position stores only `x`, `y` and `w`. The camera is a rounded rectangle or, with *Camera shape* set to *Circle*, a circle (`shape: "circle"` on the same `jsonb`; absent means rectangle, so older rows read unchanged): square in pixels, so `w` is its diameter and it may be no taller than the stage. The shape shows in the corner and custom placements, where the camera is a thumbnail, and is kept through a talking head or side by side so it comes back with the corner.

**Activities.** The presenter authors polls, quizzes (with correct answers and points), word clouds and calls to action in the host panel, each with an optional timer. A call to action is a title over the slides and a group of up to three buttons (`activities.config.buttons`, helpers in `apps/web/src/lib/cta.ts`), each opening a Stripe checkout, an embedded page or a new tab; the first is in the room's accent. Rows from before groups hold one destination and target at the top of `config` and read as a single button; a tap logs which button (`cta_events.button_index`). Activating one inserts an `activity_run`; a trigger sets `closes_at` from the timer. Participants answer in an overlay that is never taller than the screen: it is capped at the height of the layer it sits in and scrolls inside, and on phones (below `lg`) that layer is the viewport rather than the short slide strip, so a long quiz is reached by scrolling the card instead of being clipped. The database rejects answers after the timer or after the presenter closes the run, and scores quizzes server-side. "Show results to participants" flips `results_shown_at` and the overlay switches to tallies, the word cloud, or the quiz leaderboard. "Hide from participants" sets `hidden_at` and "Show to participants" clears it, at any time and as often as needed (a call to action has only these two); the run keeps its answers either way, and activating from the library starts a fresh run. Participants fetch an activity with its run, so one created or edited while they were already in the room still shows.

**Realtime.** One broadcast channel per session. Clients never subscribe to `postgres_changes`; a trigger calls `realtime.broadcast_changes` on every write and the client applies a reducer (`apps/web/src/lib/session-state.ts`). The snapshot is taken once the channel is subscribed and again after every reconnect, since broadcasts missed while offline never replay; viewers subscribe only once their anonymous identity exists, because the private channel and most tables need it. Participants delay slide changes and activity openings by the transport's live-edge latency so they land with the video: 0 over WebRTC, the player's estimate on the HLS fallback, 0 while nothing is playing.

**Chat.** One message stream per session, next to Q&A: attendees chat while the doors are open and during the session, hosts chat from the live panel. In the live room the chat and Q&A panel is as tall as the stage beside it and its list scrolls inside, with the composer fixed at the bottom; on phones it follows the stage. Messages are rows in `chat_messages` and arrive through the same broadcast; the database fills the org, trims the body, only accepts messages from someone in the room while the session is open, and holds a sender to five messages in ten seconds. The host hides a message (it leaves every screen; its author sees it was removed; the host can restore it) and switches chat off per room in Settings.

**Reactions.** Like, heart, laugh, sad, wow, clap. Taps batch client-side and land as one row per second per kind through `bump_reaction()`, which also keeps a count per attendee (`participant_reactions`) for the insights; everyone sees them float.

**Insights.** The host panel's Insights tab (Pro rooms; others see what it would show and the step up) reads a webinar two ways (helpers in `apps/web/src/lib/insights.ts`). *Registered against attended*, per event: everyone who registered and did not opt out, against the registrants whose identity joined the session that ran for the event (the sessions that started within twelve hours of a timed event's start, or any session while an untimed link was live), with the show-up rate. *Webinar insights*, per session: every attendee gets an engagement score (0–100, the buying-intent signal: answered the polls, asked questions, chatted, upvoted, reacted, put a hand up, tapped a call to action; a paid order scores 100; a session that offered no polls or calls to action does not hold them against anyone) and a fit score (0–100: every poll and quiz answer can carry a fit score of 0–10 next to it in Activities, `options[].fit`, and an attendee's fit is the fit of the answers they picked over the best possible across the session's fit-scored runs). The two scores split the audience four ways (high or low intent × high or low fit, thresholds the host can move), each group with the people in it, a *Copy emails* button and what to do next: reach out to the ready-to-buy, qualify the eager who do not fit, nurture the right people who were quiet, leave the watchers on the list. The counts come from one view, `session_engagement` (one row per attendee per session); team members in the room are left out. Fit scores are stored on the options, which attendees can read along with the question.

**Waiting room.** Per room: a picture or a looping video as the backdrop, music that loops on top (or the video's own sound), and text boxes positioned on a 16:9 canvas in a small slide-like editor with a sound preview. Shown while the session is `waiting` and when the room is closed; the music plays only while attendees wait and stops when the host goes live. Browsers hold sound back until the page has had a tap: attendees who come through the invite page's button already have one and hear it straight away, anyone who lands cold gets a *Tap for sound* button on the canvas, and everyone can mute it. Uploads are checked against the `media` bucket (100 MB; PNG/JPEG/WebP/GIF, MP4/WebM, MP3/M4A/OGG/WAV) before they start.

## Running it

Prerequisites: Node 22, pnpm 10, the Supabase CLI, a LiveKit Cloud project, an S3/R2 bucket with a public base URL, a Stripe account, a Resend account with a verified sending domain. Name each of them `cueline`.

On the hosted Supabase project, turn on **Allow anonymous sign-ins** (Authentication → Sign In / Providers) and leave captcha off; attendees get an anonymous identity client-side before they register, and `supabase/config.toml` only configures the local stack. If attendees see a "Could not start a session" message, it names the setting or limit that is in the way.

```bash
pnpm install
cp .env.example .env.local        # fill in the keys
supabase start && supabase db reset   # applies migrations + seed
pnpm dev                          # http://localhost:3000
```

1. Open `/signup` and make a host account (it makes your organisation too), or create a platform admin with `pnpm admin:create <email> <password> --super` (uses the service-role key from `.env.local`; it creates the user, adds them to the seeded org as owner, and with `--super` makes them a platform admin: the `/admin` portal and the Stripe mode switch). Then sign in at `/login` with email and password.
2. Open `/host`. Use the seeded *Main room* or create a new one. In the room, create an event with a start time and copy its invite link; check the Emails tab (the welcome and the four reminders are already there); upload a PDF, add a few activities, edit the waiting room.
3. Open the invite link in a private window and register with a name and email. The welcome email arrives with a personal link and a code; try the link in another browser, or `/join` with the email and code. **Open waiting room** puts the session in `waiting`; registrants see the waiting room under their registered name. **Go live** starts the stream. **Join with camera** publishes your video.
4. Navigate the deck, activate activities, reveal results, promote hands.

`pnpm video:check` asks LiveKit which rooms are open, who is publishing, and the status and error of every egress, then fetches the playlist each one wrote from `RECORDING_PUBLIC_BASE_URL` and reports the status code, CORS header and content type. Run it after "Go live" when participants see no video.

Checks: `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm test`, `pnpm build`, `pnpm db:test` (needs local Postgres 16 binaries and a non-root user, no Docker; a smoke check that returns `f` fails the run).

The Stripe webhook endpoint is `/api/stripe/webhook` (`checkout.session.*`, `customer.subscription.*`, `invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_failed`), one endpoint per Stripe mode, both pointing at the same URL. The email dispatcher cron is `/api/emails/dispatch` (daily via `vercel.json`, authorised with `CRON_SECRET`).

## Still to verify
